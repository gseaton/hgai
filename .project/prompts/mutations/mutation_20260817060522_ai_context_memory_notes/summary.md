## What Changed and Why
Created `docs/demos/demo-20260817-ai-context-memory-notes.md` with two new substantial sections placed at the top (ahead of the more conventional timing/Q&A/delivery-notes sections that mirror the prior decks' presenter-notes structure), reflecting that these two sections were the explicit point of the request rather than boilerplate:

- **"Why hypergraphs specifically fit agentic AI"** was built around one central, reusable framing — that an agent's own unit of work (a tool call, a conclusion, a reasoning step) is already an N-ary fact with multiple simultaneous participants (actor, input, output, evidence, confidence, session), which is exactly the shape a hyperedge holds natively and exactly the shape a binary graph edge or relational row cannot hold without either dropping participants or exploding into a small constellation of linked records. Four supporting arguments were added underneath it (MCP/protocol alignment, precision-over-recall retrieval economics under context-window scarcity, point-in-time correctness for concurrent/async multi-agent systems, and symmetric agent-writable/human-writable knowledge) — each chosen because it's specific to *agentic* systems rather than being a generic "structured data beats unstructured data" argument that would apply to any knowledge-storage pitch.
- **"How agentic AI should model knowledge to fit"** was written as five ordered, actionable heuristics (find the unit of reasoning; count real participants honestly; give reusable facts their own typed node rather than a string attribute; timestamp instead of overwrite; attach provenance/confidence to the fact itself) rather than as abstract principles, because the intended reader is someone who will leave the talk and immediately try to design a schema — each heuristic was phrased as something directly actionable during that design process, closing with an explicit "migration trigger" sentence (the moment you're about to store an agent's output as an unstructured blob, stop and ask what 2–4 entities it's really about) meant to be quotable/memorable in a live presentation.
