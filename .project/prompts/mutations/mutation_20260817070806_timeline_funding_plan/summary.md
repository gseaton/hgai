# Mutation Summary

## Intent
This request is a significant departure from every prior task in this session (which built technical rebuild specs, business-development slide decks, and an AI-memory presentation) into pure startup business/financial planning: the user wants a single document that ties together technical delivery milestones, a staffing plan with real dollar figures, a funding requirement model at six specific time checkpoints, a seed round valuation and ownership recommendation, a company legal structure recommendation, a go-to-market strategy, and adoption/revenue projections backed by market analytics — essentially the skeleton of a real fundraising planning document for HypergraphAI as a company, not just a product.

## Context
- The user supplied hard constraints to build around: a 3-month technical delivery timeline to release an open-source "hgx HypergraphX v0.1" core with "HypergraphAI v0.1" shipping as a module on it (a new strategic detail — an open-core split — not previously established anywhere in this repo or this session); alpha commercial hosting and vendor/partner training programs at month 6–9; GA of commercial hosting at month 12–15; a $3M seed ask; and a specific 10-role staffing plan (CEO, CTO/Tech Lead/Chief Scientist, a separately-listed Tech Lead, 1–4 developers ramping to the 3-month mark, COO/CFO, CPO/Sales/BD, Admin) that was interpreted as the CTO being strategic/architecture/research-focused while a separate Tech Lead handles day-to-day engineering management of the developer pool — a reading stated explicitly in the document rather than left ambiguous.
- The user asked for "supporting market analytics" and valuation estimates for actual company fundraising use — given the stakes of a real financial document, this session loaded the WebSearch tool and ran three targeted queries (knowledge graph market sizing, 2026 AI seed valuation/round-size data, enterprise AI agent and MCP adoption statistics) rather than reusing the existing `docs/investor-pitch-deck.md`'s unsourced figures (e.g. its uncited "78% of enterprises deploying AI agents by 2026 (Gartner)" claim) or fabricating new numbers from training-data recall. The search results were cited by URL directly in the new document's Section 9, with an explicit caveat that provider estimates vary widely (knowledge graph market 2026 estimates ranged $1.9B–$2.9B depending on source) rather than presenting a single cherry-picked figure as fact.
- The open-core product split described by the user ("hgx" open source core + "HypergraphAI" as a commercial module on top of it) was recognized as directly compatible with — not requiring any redesign of — this codebase's existing modular architecture (`hgai_module_*` packages mounted conditionally at startup, already documented in project memory as a core design principle). This compatibility was called out explicitly in the document as a de-risking point for the open-core strategy.

## What Changed and Why
Created `docs/operations/timeline-funding-20260817.md` (and its parent `docs/operations/` folder, new in this repo) as a single, internally-consistent planning document. The core analytical work, done before writing prose, was building an actual bottom-up financial model rather than asserting round numbers:

- **Staffing/burn model**: assigned illustrative but reasoned market-rate base salaries to all 10 roles, applied a standard 1.3x burden multiplier, built a hire-date ramp reaching the full team by month 3 (matching the stated developer ramp), and phased non-personnel costs (infra, legal, insurance, marketing, security/compliance) to scale with company stage rather than holding them flat. This produced real, computed cumulative-spend figures at all six requested checkpoints, not placeholder numbers.
- **Revenue model**: built bottom-up from the stated alpha (month 6–9) and GA (month 12–15) milestones with explicit, stated customer-count and pricing assumptions (illustrative $1.5K/$5K/$15K hosting tiers blending to a stated MRR curve), rather than working backward from a target ARR — deliberately modeling a "growth off a small base" curve that stays modest through the alpha phase and only accelerates after GA removes the "still in alpha" objection, since an unrealistically fast early ramp would have undermined the document's credibility.
- **The central finding, computed rather than assumed**: comparing cumulative burn against cumulative projected revenue at each checkpoint showed that $3M in pure burn (zero revenue credit) funds the company to approximately month 17 — comfortably past GA — while net-of-projected-revenue burn stays just under $3M through month 24. This let the document make a specific, actionable recommendation (treat 17 months as the real risk-managed runway trigger for starting Series A conversations, treat the longer revenue-netted runway as upside/term-improvement rather than a solvency plan) instead of either overstating how far $3M stretches or leaving runway unaddressed.
- **Valuation recommendation**: rather than anchoring to the freshly-retrieved 2026 AI-sector seed median (~$17.9M–$24M pre-money across sources), the document explicitly argues for pricing below that market-wide AI-hype median — given this company's pre-revenue, ten-person, single-market-infrastructure profile — and recommends an $11M pre-money / $14M post-money / ~21.4% new-investor-ownership structure, with an explicit note on how a standard option-pool top-up affects real founder dilution beyond the headline investor percentage.
- **Company structure**: built a comparison ruling out LLP and S-Corp as fundamentally incompatible with VC investment mechanics (no preferred stock, shareholder caps, pass-through tax complications for institutional LPs), positioned LLC as a pre-raise-only option that should convert to a Delaware C-Corp before a priced round, and — given this project's own established open-source/AI-alignment framing found in prior design materials — presented Delaware Public Benefit Corporation as a serious, equally fundable alternative to a standard C-Corp rather than a purely symbolic option, since PBCs use identical stock/QSBS mechanics.
- **GTM strategy**: built as three sequential motions (OSS product-led adoption → direct commercial hosting → partner/training channel) mapped directly onto the open-core structure from Section 2, and explicitly cross-referenced the two defense-industry business-development decks already created earlier in this session (`demo-bus-dev-20260817`, `demo-spec-ops-20260817`) as concrete, already-built evidence that the partner-channel motion isn't hypothetical for this project.
- **Risks/assumptions section**: closed the document by naming its own most load-bearing and speculative assumption (the revenue ramp curve) explicitly, along with GA-timing slip risk, the fractional-CFO cost-saving option left unmodeled by default, and Section 9's own data point that Gartner projects 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by 2027 — included specifically so the document doesn't read as uncritically bullish given its own market research.

## Key Decisions
- **Grounded every market/valuation claim in live-searched, cited 2026 data** rather than reusing the existing investor deck's unsourced figures or generating plausible-sounding numbers from training recall — judged necessary because this document is explicitly positioned for potential real fundraising use, where an uncited or stale statistic is a credibility risk in a way it wasn't for the earlier internal-audience decks.
- **Explicitly flagged provider disagreement in market sizing** (showing a range rather than a single figure) and added a caveat against treating the knowledge-graph market as an uncritical TAM proxy — chosen over presenting a single, cleaner-sounding number, on the judgment that a document meant to inform real decisions should surface the genuine uncertainty in its own inputs rather than hide it for narrative smoothness.
- **Recommended pricing the seed round below the current AI-sector hype median** rather than anchoring to the highest defensible market comparable — the document states this reasoning explicitly (avoiding a difficult flat/down Series A from an overpriced, traction-light seed) rather than just picking a number, since the user asked for a "common sense" figure, not a maximized one.
- **Computed and reported the burn-only vs. revenue-netted runway split explicitly**, and made a specific recommendation about which one to treat as the real decision-relevant number for fundraising timing — this was the single most analytically important choice in the document, since simply presenting the more optimistic revenue-netted 24-month figure alone would have been more flattering but a materially riskier planning assumption to hand to founders.
