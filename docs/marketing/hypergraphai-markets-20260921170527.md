# HypergraphAI - Markets: TAM, SAM and SOM by Product Space and Use Case

**Generated:** 2026-09-21 · **Horizon:** 2030 (year 5) · **Currency:** nominal USD · **Status:** planning estimate - desk research only, **not yet validated by primary research**

---

## 1. Overview

**Purpose.** This document does two things: it (1) sets out the **plan** for determining the approximate total, serviceable and obtainable markets for HypergraphAI, and (2) applies that plan to produce a **first-pass estimate** for every product space and horizontal use case identified so far - each with its own TAM / SAM / SOM - plus an **aggregate**, de-duplicated across use cases.

**Scope.** 24 use cases in four groups:

| Group | Use cases |
|---|---|
| **The seven spaces named in the request** | 1 personal knowledge store · 2 enterprise knowledge store · 3 personal analytics · 4 enterprise analytics · 5 enterprise semantic integration layer · 6 agent persisted memory across vendors / models / hosts / sessions · 7 agentic semantic knowledge store |
| **Other horizontal spaces** | 8 general-purpose graph / n-ary data store · 9 metadata, governance & provenance · 10 master data & entity resolution · 11 ontology & taxonomy management · 12 enterprise search augmentation |
| **Vertical solution spaces** | 13 fraud / AML · 14 cyber threat intelligence · 15 supply chain & provenance · 16 life sciences · 17 GRC / RegTech / legal · 18 defense & intelligence · 19 software-engineering & architecture knowledge |
| **First-of-kind / emerging** | 20 portable user-owned AI memory · 21 multi-agent shared world model · 22 agent accountability ledger · 23 federated knowledge mesh & brokering · 24 point-in-time decision reproducibility |

**Definitions (used throughout).**

| Term | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **TAM** | Annual spend in **2030** on the product category in which a semantic knowledge / hypergraph store could be the primary platform. Top-down from analyst reports (parent market × the slice where a knowledge store is the natural architecture), or bottom-up (population × price) where no report exists. |
| **SAM** | The part of TAM HypergraphAI can serve with its business model - open-core, self-hosted or hosted, MCP-first, English-first, an enterprise sale supported by design partners and partners. **Always bottom-up:** target organizations (or users) × share with the need × share reachable × annual contract value (ACV). |
| **SOM** | Annual recurring revenue realistically obtainable by **year 5 (2030)**: SAM × obtainable share. Marketplace revenue is counted at the platform's take rate. |
| **Anchor / core wedge** | Use cases 6 and 7 - the agentic memory and knowledge stores that the investor deck's SAM and SOM are built on. |
| **Aggregate** | Across all use cases, **after** removing overlap (the same budget or account counted in several use cases) and, for SOM, after applying an **execution constraint** (a single company cannot win 24 markets at once). |

**How to read the numbers.** Every figure is the output of stated inputs (Sections 3 and 8); each card in Sections 5 and 6 shows its inputs and a conservative / base / optimistic range. Analyst market sizes for the same category routinely differ by 2–10× (see the parent-market table in Section 4), so **ranges matter more than point estimates**, and none of this should be quoted externally until the validation work in Section 3.6 is done.

### 2.4 Key findings

1. **The aggregate net TAM is about $28.0B ($16.0B–$49.1B) in 2030 - but almost none of it is "the" market.** It is a stack of 24 slices of much larger parent markets (fraud alone is $66B–$150B, MDM $39B–$54B). Only the slices where a knowledge / relationship store is the natural architecture are counted, and the result is de-duplicated. The investor deck's TAM of $6–14B covers only knowledge-graph platforms plus agent memory; this analysis widens the lens.
2. **The obtainable market is concentrated in the agentic core.** Use cases 6 and 7 carry $29M of base SOM (79% of the execution-constrained aggregate) and closely reproduce the investor deck's SAM ($486M against ≈$0.49B) and SOM ($29M against ≈$28M), because they are built from the same assumptions.
3. **Headline SOM is $37M (year-5 ARR), $12M–$80M across scenarios.** That is about $7.7M above the deck's core case, contributed by adjacent spaces (enterprise knowledge stores, fraud, defense, engineering knowledge, multi-agent state, agent accountability). **The seed plan does not fund those**; treat the excess as upside that depends on packs, connectors and partners being built.
4. **The unconstrained SOM ($58M) shows the opportunity cost of focus**, not a forecast: $21M of obtainable revenue sits in spaces the company cannot staff in its first five years.
5. **First-of-kind spaces (20–24) are real but unproven markets.** Base TAM $4.7B / SAM $177M / SOM $6.2M, built from analogues (paid AI subscribers, multi-agent platforms, AI-governance spend, data marketplaces, RegTech) rather than reports about the category itself; confidence is Low to Very low. Their value is strategic - category creation and defensibility - more than near-term revenue. Two claims of being first are strong (20, 23), two moderate (21, 22) and one weak (24).
6. **Every SAM is far below its TAM (0.7%–12%).** That is deliberate: HypergraphAI is a new open-core entrant without SSO, SOC 2, connectors, vector search or a hosted GA today. Closing those gaps raises reachability more than any other lever.
7. **The largest uncertainties are inputs, not arithmetic:** the number of large enterprises, the share running production agents by 2030, enterprise ACV, and the obtainable share (Section 8). Halving the obtainable share roughly halves SOM; a lower agent-adoption rate hits the core hardest.

## 3. The Plan - How TAM, SAM and SOM Are Determined

### 3.1 Principles

1. **One definition per layer, applied identically to every use case** (Section 1). No use case gets a bespoke method except where no analyst data exists, and then it is labelled bottom-up.
2. **SAM and SOM are always bottom-up.** Analyst reports size TAM; they cannot tell us what an open-core entrant can reach.
3. **Show ranges, not points.** Conservative / base / optimistic scenarios come from the *spread of sources* (TAM) and from explicit low / base / high inputs (SAM, SOM).
4. **Every assumption is named, numbered and has a validation method** (Section 10).
5. **Aggregate honestly.** Remove double counting, and cap SOM by what can actually be executed.
6. **Confidence is graded per use case**, and novelty is penalised: markets with no analyst coverage are "Low" or "Very low" regardless of how large they look.

### 3.2 Method, step by step

| Step | Activity | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Inventory use cases** from the product's capabilities, existing decks and the request; assign each to a cluster (A knowledge stores · B agent context · C analytics · D integration & governance · E verticals · F ecosystem) and a go-to-market tier | Use-case catalog (Section 5) |
| 2 | **Identify the parent market(s)** for each use case; collect **three or more independent 2026 / 2030 estimates**; normalise to 2030 USD (back-cast 2031–2035 forecasts with the stated CAGR); discard scope outliers with the reason recorded | Parent-market table (Section 4) |
| 3 | **Apply the slice** - the fraction of the parent market where a knowledge / relationship store is the natural architecture - with low / base / high values and a stated rationale | TAM per use case |
| 4 | **Where no report exists** - including first-of-kind categories - build TAM bottom-up: population × annual price, or an analogous market × the share that the new category would take (Section 6) | TAM (labelled bottom-up / analogue) |
| 5 | **Build SAM bottom-up** from segments: N target organizations or users × *p* (share with the need) × *r* (share reachable by our channels and product today) × ACV | SAM per use case |
| 6 | **Set the obtainable share** *s* using comparables (Section 3.3) and the competitive intensity of the space | SOM per use case |
| 7 | **Grade confidence and novelty** (Section 3.4) | Confidence label per card |
| 8 | **Aggregate**: cluster de-duplication for TAM; anchor-plus-incremental for SAM and SOM; tier-based execution factors for SOM (Section 7) | Aggregate TAM / SAM / SOM |
| 9 | **Stress-test**: one-at-a-time sensitivities and the three scenarios | Section 8 |
| 10 | **Validate with primary research**, replace assumptions with evidence, re-run | Section 3.6 |

**Formulas**

```
TAM(use case)  = Σ over components ( parent market_2030 × slice )        or   population × annual price
SAM(use case)  = Σ over segments   ( N × p × r × ACV )
SOM(use case)  = SAM × s × net          (net = platform take rate for the marketplace use case, else 1)

TAM(aggregate) = 0.85 × Σ over clusters ( retention_cluster × Σ TAM(use cases in cluster) )
SAM(aggregate) = Σ SAM(Tier 1) + 0.75 × Σ SAM(Tier 2 and 3)
SOM(aggregate) = Σ SOM(Tier 1) + 0.75 × Σ ( SOM × tier factor )         tier factor: Tier 2 = 0.5, Tier 3 = 0.2
```

### 3.3 Data sources and comparables

| Need | Source (in priority order) |
|---|---|
| Parent-market sizes | Published analyst estimates (three or more per category, retrieved 2026-09-21); purchase two full reports for the categories that matter most (knowledge graph, agent memory / orchestration, graph database) |
| Population counts | Firmographic data (large-enterprise counts by industry / region), developer surveys, platform statistics |
| Price points | Own pricing plan (Cloud $1.5K / $5K / $15K+ per month), competitor list prices (Neo4j AuraDB is capacity-priced, Mem0 free-to-$249 per month, Zep from $25 per month) |
| Obtainable share | Comparables: Neo4j about $200M ARR after more than a decade [S58, S59]; TigerGraph ($105M Series C in 2021), Stardog (about $32M raised), TypeDB (about $11M raised) [S60, S61, S62]; Mem0 ($24M raised) and Letta ($10M seed) [S63]; the investor deck's own ramp ($2.2M ARR at year 2 → $28M at year 5) |
| Adoption drivers | Gartner (17% of organizations had deployed agents in its 2026 CIO survey; 40% of enterprises expected to use GraphRAG by 2029; over half of agent systems expected to use context graphs by 2028) [S66, S64, S65]; MCP adoption [S69]; EU AI Act logging duties [S70] |

### 3.4 Confidence rubric

| Grade | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **High** | Three or more independent analyst sources agree within ±30%; the bottom-up has been checked against primary evidence |
| **Medium** | Analysts cover the category directly (or a well-covered parent with an assumed slice of 20% or more); low and high sources within about 3×; SAM rests on sourced adoption drivers |
| **Low** | The TAM is a small assumed slice (under 20%) of a larger parent, or sources disagree by more than 3×, or the population is itself an assumption |
| **Very low** | No analyst coverage of the category (first-of-kind spaces with a strong or moderate claim); sized by analogy |

No use case is rated High today, because no primary research has been done.

### 3.5 Tiering and the execution constraint

| Tier | Rule | Execution factor |
|---|---|---:|
| **1 - core wedge** | In the seed plan; the product already fits; buyer and budget identified | 1.0 |
| **2 - adjacent** | Same buyers or platform; needs a pack, connector or partner after GA | 0.5 |
| **3 - option** | Weak fit today, low SAM, or unproven category; pursue opportunistically | 0.2 |

A use case moves **up a tier** when all of these hold: three or more design partners with a budget owner; base SAM ≥ $100M; base SOM ≥ $5M; product fit rated strong with two quarters or less of build. It **moves down or is dropped** if two consecutive validation rounds show no budget owner.

### 3.6 Work plan and validation (about 10 weeks)

| Phase | Weeks | Activities | Deliverable / exit criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 - Freeze** | 0–1 | Freeze definitions, use-case list, model template (this document's model) and the assumption register (Section 10) | Signed-off definitions |
| **1 - Secondary research** | 1–3 | Collect ≥3 estimates per parent market; buy the two priority reports; record scope differences; competitor revenue / funding triangulation; refresh Gartner / IDC adoption data | Parent-market table with every source and outlier decision |
| **2 - Bottom-up build** | 2–5 | Firmographic counts (large enterprises by industry and region); adoption curves for production agents and KG programmes; ACV benchmarks; developer-funnel benchmarks for open-core conversion | Population and ACV inputs replaced or confirmed |
| **3 - Primary validation** | 4–8 | **30–40 buyer interviews** stratified across use cases (at least 5 each for 6, 7, 2, 13, 19); **150–300-respondent survey** of AI-platform and data leaders (need, budget, vendor shortlist); **pricing research** (Gabor-Granger or Van Westendorp); **10 design-partner conversations** aiming at 3 letters of intent; 6–8 analyst / expert calls; instrument open-source download → active → paid funnel | Evidence file per use case; confidence upgraded where earned |
| **4 - Integrate and stress-test** | 7–9 | Re-run the model; overlap review with sales; sensitivity and (optionally) Monte-Carlo simulation; **red-team review** by someone outside the product team | Revised Sections 2, 5, 7, 8 |
| **5 - Publish and operate** | 10 | Publish v2; agree tier changes; set a **quarterly refresh** and **event-driven refreshes** (major model-vendor memory launches, competitor funding rounds, regulation dates, first paid conversions) | Approved market model and refresh calendar |

**Indicative budget:** $20–50K (two analyst reports, a panel survey, an expert network, interview incentives) plus about 0.5 FTE of founder / product time. This is a planning estimate.

**Roles:** an accountable owner (CEO or head of product); a researcher (internal or contracted); one sales or partnerships lead for interviews and design partners; an independent reviewer for the red team.

**Acceptance criteria for v2:** every base input traced to a source or an evidence file; at least half of the Tier 1 and Tier 2 use cases at Medium confidence or better; a written rationale for every tier change; a reconciliation to the financial model in the funding plan.

Before any of these is described externally as "first" or "only", the plan requires a **competitive scan** (Phase 1) recording, for each space: existing products within two feature gaps; whether an incumbent has announced it; and the evidence for user demand. The current assessment:

### 6.4 Why they are still worth carrying

- **They are adjacent to the core buyers.** The same organizations that adopt the agentic memory and knowledge stores (6, 7) need agent accountability (22) and shared state (21) as deployments grow; the EU AI Act's logging duties for high-risk systems start in August 2026 [S70].
- **They create defensibility.** Portability (20) and knowledge brokering (23) are network-effect plays; the mesh, spaces, export/import and provenance primitives already exist.
- **Category-creation risk is real.** Base SAM for the five spaces is only $177M; they should be funded as experiments with explicit kill criteria (Section 3.5), not as a revenue plan.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A - Knowledge & semantic stores | 5 | 60% | $9.5B | $5.7B |
| B - Agent context, knowledge & accountability | 5 | 70% | $12.3B | $8.6B |
| C - Analytics | 2 | 80% | $6.4B | $5.1B |
| D - Integration, governance & provenance | 4 | 65% | $9.5B | $6.1B |
| E - Vertical solutions | 7 | 85% | $8.2B | $7.0B |
| F - Knowledge ecosystem | 1 | 100% | $390M | $390M |
| **Total** | 24 | | **$46.2B** | **$32.9B** |

After the 0.85 cross-cluster factor: **net TAM $28.0B** (conservative $16.0B, optimistic $49.1B). Retention factors are judgement: they are lowest where products substitute for each other (clusters A and D) and highest where budgets are distinct (verticals E, ecosystem F).

### 7.2 SAM and SOM: one platform, many uses

The same account rarely pays twice for a platform. **Tier 1 (the anchors, 6 and 7) is counted in full** because it is built to reconcile to the investor deck; every other use case counts **75%** of its SAM and SOM as incremental. SOM is then multiplied by the tier's **execution factor** (Tier 2: 50%, Tier 3: 20%).

## 11. Limitations

- **Desk research only.** No customers, prospects or experts were interviewed; every input is an estimate.
